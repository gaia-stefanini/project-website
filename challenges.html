Challenges faced & solutions

  • Understanding SPARQL queries: understanding how to write correct SPARQL queries was the first challenge we faced during the project. Despite a relatively simple logical structure, a small mistake in the use of a prefix, punctuation mark, or query pattern can lead to different results or query failures, and identifying such mistakes wasn’t always easy. Starting with simpler queries and using LLMs proved to be an effective solution.

  • Virtuoso time out: multiple times during the development of the project, the ArCo SPARQL Editor returned timeout messages, mostly with more complex queries, but not only. Since this problem depended on the platform and it couldn’t be solved, carrying out the research required a lot more time than anticipated.

  • Creating the website: since we were beginners in web development, this step was particularly challenging. We wanted to create our own unique website instead of using a template, but it proved to be more difficult than we thought. In order to do it, we carefully studied Github instructions and used tools such as LLMs to help us write the code needed to structure our website.

  • One of the main solutions to solve the various challenges encountered was to rely on the teaching materials available on Virtuale and also on the LLMs.


    • Conclusion

    The proposed enrichments address several aspects of the ArCo and Wikidata knowledge graphs, including textual descriptions, commissioners, artists and sculptors, labels, and links connecting the two churches to related entities. For the specific resources investigated, much of this information was either missing or not sufficiently represented. The RDF triples created in this project therefore contribute to improving the completeness, consistency, and semantic richness of the datasets, providing a clearer and more detailed representation of both the Basilica di San Domenico and the Basilica di San Petronio.

    Overall, the investigation of these two important religious monuments of Bologna demonstrates the value of knowledge graphs for representing and connecting cultural heritage information. It also shows how SPARQL can be used to identify gaps in existing datasets and how new RDF triples can be proposed to address those gaps. In addition, the use of LLMs supported the process of identifying relevant information and transforming it into structured semantic data. The combination of semantic technologies and LLMs can therefore contribute to making cultural heritage knowledge graphs more complete, expressive, and useful, while facilitating connections between monuments, people, institutions, and other related entities.
